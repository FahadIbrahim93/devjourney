# Storyteller

## Purpose
Tell stories that stick — personal narrative, brand story, customer narrative. Stories drive action. Facts tell, stories sell.

## Trigger Conditions
- Brand needs origin story
- Personal bio / About page
- Customer testimonial (turn into narrative)
- Pitch / sales narrative
- On-demand via `/storyteller <purpose>`

## Required Inputs
- **Story type** (brand origin, personal, customer, future)
- **Subject** (what / who is the story about)
- **Audience** (who needs to hear this)
- **Goal** (what action should it drive)
- **Length** (50 words, 500 words, 2,000 words)

## Steps

### 1. Story Type Selection

**Origin story (brand):** "How we came to be"
**Personal story (you):** "Who I am, why I do this"
**Customer story:** "How our product changed their life"
**Future story:** "What we're building toward"
**Manifesto:** "What we believe"

### 2. The 7-Element Story Framework

Every good story has 7 elements (Pixar + classic narrative):

1. **You (before):** Where you were, the normal state
2. **Want:** What you / the character wanted
3. **Conflict:** What stood in the way
4. **Struggle:** The hard work / failure
5. **Turning point:** The moment it changed
6. **Transformation:** What you / character became
7. **Lesson:** What we can learn

### 3. The "Why" Test

A great story answers: "Why should I care?"

For each story, find the **universal truth** it points to:
- "Starting is the hardest part"
- "Persistence beats talent"
- "We all want to be seen"
- "Small acts matter"
- "Trust is everything"

**Rule:** If the lesson is generic, dig deeper.

### 4. Specificity Over Abstraction

**Abstract:** "I worked hard to build a successful business."
**Specific:** "In April 2024, I shipped my first feature at 2 AM after 11 failed attempts. The next morning, 23 people used it. I cried."

**Abstract:** "We value craftsmanship."
**Specific:** "Our metalsmith spends 8 hours on a single cuff. He could do it in 3 if he used a machine. He won't."

Specificity = credibility = emotion.

### 5. Show, Don't Tell

**Telling:** "I was scared."
**Showing:** "My hands were shaking so hard I spilled coffee on the keyboard."

**Telling:** "Our jewelry is beautiful."
**Showing:** "When she put on the cuff, the room went quiet for a second. Then her friend said, 'Oh, that's a Marjahans piece, isn't it?'"

### 6. Structure Templates

**Template A: Hero's Journey (long form)**
1. Ordinary world
2. Call to adventure
3. Refusal
4. Mentor / helper
5. Crossing the threshold
6. Trials
7. Revelation
8. Transformation
9. Return with elixir

**Template B: Before/After (testimonial)**
1. The "before" state (specific pain)
2. Discovery (how they found you)
3. The change (what happened)
4. The "after" state (specific transformation)

**Template C: Three-Act (pitch)**
1. The world today (context)
2. The problem (gap, pain)
3. Your solution (the future)

**Template D: List (threads)**
- "3 lessons from launching a brand in 90 days"
- Each lesson = mini-story

### 7. Story Channels

**Long-form (1,500+ words):**
- Substack
- Personal blog
- LinkedIn long-form post
- Book chapters

**Medium (300-800 words):**
- IG carousel
- LinkedIn post
- Email newsletter
- About page

**Short (50-150 words):**
- IG caption
- Tweet thread (one per tweet)
- Bio / elevator pitch
- Product description

**Spoken (1-5 min):**
- Podcast intro
- Video intro
- Sales call opening
- Conference talk

### 8. Revise + Polish

**Pass 1: Cut filler**
- Remove "I was," "I had been," "It was"
- Cut every sentence that doesn't advance the story

**Pass 2: Strengthen verbs**
- "Walked" → "strode" (or "trudged")
- "Said" → "whispered" (or "shouted")

**Pass 3: Add sensory details**
- What did you see, hear, smell, taste, touch?
- Specific = real

**Pass 4: Read aloud**
- If it sounds clunky, rewrite
- Real conversation > written prose

**Pass 5: Check for AI-tells**
- No perfect parallel structure
- No "Let's dive in"
- No "In conclusion"
- Real stories are messy, not neat

## Output Format

```markdown
📖 **STORY: "The First Cuff" (Marjahans Brand Origin)**

**Type:** Brand origin
**Length:** 600 words
**Audience:** New customers, IG bio, About page
**Goal:** Connect, establish craft heritage, build trust

---

**Story:**

I was 19 when my aunt handed me a brass cuff, tarnished almost black with age.

"Tell me what you see," she said.

I saw an old bracelet. Ugly, even. The kind of thing you'd find in a thrift store for $5.

She smiled. "Look closer."

I did. And I noticed the patina wasn't random — it was the result of decades of touch, of skin oils and rain and Dhaka dust. The brass was 80% copper, which meant it had been alive, oxidizing, evolving with whoever wore it.

"This isn't a piece of jewelry," she said. "It's a record. A record of all the people who've touched it."

I sold that cuff a year later to a museum curator in London. She paid $400 for it. I cried in the bathroom afterward — not from sadness, but from the realization that what we make in Bangladesh, what our families have made for generations, has real value. Real cultural value. Real craft value.

**Marjahans started the next year.**

We don't make jewelry. We make records. Each piece is a story waiting to be told — by the person who wears it.

We use traditional techniques, sourced from artisans who've been doing this for 30+ years. We don't mass-produce. We don't use machines to "improve" efficiency. We use hands.

When you wear a Marjahans piece, you're not just wearing jewelry. You're wearing 30 years of someone's craft. You're writing a new chapter in a record that began before you were born.

And someday, you'll pass it on. And someone will look at it and say, "Tell me what you see."

---

**Variations:**

**100-word version (IG bio):**
"I was 19 when my aunt handed me a brass cuff. 'Look closer,' she said. I noticed the patina — 30 years of touch. We don't make jewelry. We make records. Each piece is a story waiting to be told. Marjahans: handcrafted brass, 80% copper, alive. Dhaka → your wrist."

**30-second spoken (podcast intro):**
"Growing up in Dhaka, I learned that jewelry isn't decoration. It's a record. My aunt showed me a 30-year-old cuff, and the patina told me about everyone who'd touched it. That's why I started Marjahans — to make records, not accessories. Each piece is alive."

**Testimonial version (template for customers):**
"When I first put on [piece], my friend noticed. She asked where I got it. I told her: Marjahans. She said, 'Oh, that brand. I've been looking at their pieces for months.' Two weeks later, she bought the matching one. Now we have a tradition: every birthday, a new Marjahans piece."
```

## Common Pitfalls
- ❌ Generic story ("we wanted to make something better")
- ❌ Telling, not showing
- ❌ No conflict (perfect rise = boring)
- ❌ "We're like [other brand]" (derivative)
- ❌ Too long (people won't read)
- ❌ No revision (first draft is never good)
- ❌ AI-tells (sounds like ChatGPT)
- ❌ No "why" (just "what" you do)
