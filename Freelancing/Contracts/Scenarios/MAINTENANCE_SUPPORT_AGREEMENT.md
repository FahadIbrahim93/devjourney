## MAINTENANCE & SUPPORT BEST PRACTICES

### Setting Up the Agreement:
- Clearly define what's included vs. what requires additional authorization
- Establish realistic response and resolution times based on your capacity
- Consider seasonal business cycles when setting monthly hours
- Include provisions for scope creep prevention
- Set up proper access controls and security measures from day one
- Define clear escalation paths for complex issues

### Delivering Excellent Support:
- Implement a ticketing or tracking system (even if simple)
- Standardize response templates for common issues
- Document solutions for future reference and knowledge base
- Communicate proactively about status and delays
- Follow up after resolution to ensure satisfaction
- Look for patterns in issues to suggest preventive measures
- Maintain professional demeanor even during stressful situations

### Managing Client Expectations:
- Be honest about limitations and response times
- Educate clients on what constitutes an emergency vs. routine issue
- Provide regular health reports even when no issues occur
- Suggest improvements during routine maintenance work
- Be transparent about third-party dependencies and limitations
- Push back appropriately on unreasonable demands

### Pricing & Profitability:
- Track actual time spent vs. allocated time to optimize tiers
- Consider value-based pricing for clients with high downtime costs
- Offer discounts for long-term commitments (6+ months)
- Include provisions for annual price adjustments
- Monitor profitability per client and adjust tiers as needed
- Consider bundling with other services for increased value

### Red Flags in Support Relationships:
- Client consistently using emergency support for non-emergencies
- Refusal to follow established support procedures
- Constantly requesting work outside scope without approving charges
- Unwillingness to provide necessary access or information
- History of late payments or disputing charges
- Expectation of 24/7 instant response without appropriate tier
- Pressure to bypass security protocols or best practices
- Blaming support for issues caused by client's own actions

### Scaling Your Support Business:
- Create knowledge base articles for common issues
- Develop standardized troubleshooting procedures
- Consider tiered support staff (junior/senior) as you grow
- Implement monitoring and alerting for proactive issue detection
- Develop relationships with specialized subcontractors
- Create service level agreements with clear metrics
- Regularly review and update your service offerings
- Consider creating industry-specific support packages
